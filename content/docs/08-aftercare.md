# Aftercare

## After a filling

If you had local anaesthetic, avoid eating until the numbness has gone, usually two to
three hours. The commonest injury after a filling is a bitten lip or cheek in a child who
ate too soon.

Sensitivity to cold for a few days is normal and settles. Sensitivity that lingers more than
two weeks, pain that wakes you at night, or pain on biting that is getting worse after a
week, is not normal and you should call us. It usually means the bite needs adjusting, which
takes five minutes, or that the nerve is unhappy, which needs a proper look.

A white filling is fully set before you leave, so you can eat on it as soon as the numbness
has gone.

## After an extraction

Do not rinse at all for 24 hours. Rinsing washes out the clot and a lost clot is what
causes a dry socket, which is the painful complication people remember.

After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salty water four times a day for a week, a teaspoon
of salt in a mug of warm water.

No smoking for 72 hours, no alcohol for 24 hours, nothing hot to drink on the day, and no
strenuous exercise for 48 hours. Some oozing on the first evening is expected and looks
worse mixed with saliva than it is.

If bleeding restarts, roll the gauze into a firm pad, place it over the socket and bite
firmly for 30 minutes without checking it. If it is still bleeding heavily after an hour of
proper pressure, call the practice, or go to A&E if we are closed.

A dry socket typically starts three to four days afterwards as a deep ache that painkillers
barely touch, often with a bad taste. Call us. It is dressed in a ten-minute appointment and
the relief is immediate.

## After hygiene

Gums may be tender and may bleed a little for a day or two. Use a soft brush and keep
brushing, because stopping makes it worse and the bleeding settles within a week in almost
everyone.

Sensitivity at the necks of the teeth is common after a thorough clean, particularly the
first one after a long gap. Sensitive toothpaste rubbed on with a finger and left rather
than rinsed off works better than brushing with it.

Avoid staining food and drink for 24 hours.

## After deep cleaning

Expect the gums to be sore for two or three days and to shrink back a little over the next
few weeks, which can leave gaps between the teeth and more sensitivity. That is the
treatment working rather than a complication, but nobody enjoys it and it is better to know
in advance.

Interdental brushes are the single thing that determines whether deep cleaning holds. The
hygienist will size them for you.

## After whitening

Sensitivity for 24 to 48 hours is common and settles. Use the desensitising gel supplied,
in the trays, for 30 minutes at a time.

Stick to a white diet for the first 48 hours: no red wine, coffee, tea, curry, berries,
beetroot or tomato sauce. Smoking undoes whitening faster than anything on that list.

The shade lifts for about two weeks after you stop, so judge the result then rather than on
the last day of treatment.

## After anti-wrinkle injections

Stay upright for four hours. No exercise for 24 hours, no facials, saunas or steam rooms for
48 hours, and do not rub or massage the area.

Small raised bumps at the injection points settle within an hour. Pinpoint bruises are
common and cover easily.

The effect appears over three to seven days and is fully settled at two weeks, which is when
it is reviewed and adjusted if needed. Do not judge it at day three.

## After dermal filler

Swelling is normal and peaks at about 48 hours. Small bruises are common and can take a
week to fade. Sleep on your back for two nights and avoid make-up over the area for 12
hours.

Avoid dental treatment for two weeks, and avoid facials, saunas and sunbeds for a fortnight.

Firm lumps that are still there at two weeks should be reviewed. Blanching, meaning skin
that goes white and stays white, severe pain out of proportion to the treatment, or any
change in vision, needs to be reported immediately, on the same day, by phone.

## When to call us rather than wait

Facial swelling that is spreading, difficulty swallowing or opening the mouth, a temperature
alongside dental pain, bleeding that has not stopped after an hour of pressure, or any
vision change after filler. Those are same-day calls.

# Preparing for your appointment

## What to bring

Bring a list of any medication you take, including anything bought over the counter and any
supplements, because several of them affect bleeding and healing. Bring the name and
address of your previous dentist if you have had treatment in the last two years.

If you have had X-rays elsewhere in the last twelve months, ask that practice to email them
across and we can often avoid repeating them. Ours is a referrals address on the website and
the front desk will give it to you.

Bring your reading glasses if you use them. The consent forms and the treatment plan are
easier that way.

## Arriving

Arrive five minutes early for a routine appointment and fifteen minutes early for a first
visit, because the medical history form takes a few minutes. The form can also be completed
online from the link in the confirmation email, which is faster and means you can arrive
five minutes before instead.

The confirmation email goes out when you book and a text reminder goes out 48 hours before.
If neither arrived, check the address and number we hold, because that is usually what has
gone wrong.

## Before hygiene

Eat beforehand if you can, because the mouth is uncomfortable for a while afterwards and
because a scale and polish on an empty stomach is a longer 30 minutes than it needs to be.

Brush normally. There is no need to do anything special, and scrubbing hard beforehand
makes the gums bleed more during the appointment.

Avoid staining food and drink for 24 hours afterwards, so no red wine, coffee, tea, curry or
berries.

## Before whitening

Have a scale and polish within the two weeks before whitening starts. Whitening works on
clean enamel and the result is noticeably more even.

Use a sensitive toothpaste for two weeks beforehand if you know your teeth are sensitive. It
makes a real difference to how comfortable the first few days are.

Do not book whitening in the two weeks before a wedding or similar. Sensitivity and small
patchy areas settle in the first fortnight and you want to be past that.

## Before facial aesthetics

Avoid alcohol for 24 hours beforehand. Avoid ibuprofen, aspirin, fish oil, vitamin E and
ginkgo for 48 hours where it is safe for you to do so, because they all increase bruising.
Do not stop prescribed blood thinners without speaking to whoever prescribed them.

Do not book aesthetics within two weeks of a dental appointment involving local anaesthetic,
and do not book dental treatment within two weeks after filler.

Come without make-up if you can, or allow ten minutes to remove it at the clinic.

Do not book anything with visible downtime in the week before an event. Filler swelling
peaks at 48 hours and bruising can take a week.

## Before a child's appointment

Bring a parent or guardian who can consent. Under-16s cannot be seen without an adult with
parental responsibility present for the whole appointment. A grandparent or childminder
cannot consent unless they hold parental responsibility.

Book a morning slot if you can. Children handle dental appointments better before lunch, and
the front desk will say so if you ask.

## If you are unwell

If you have a fever, a chest infection, or anything contagious, call and move the
appointment. There is no charge for cancelling because you are ill, at any notice.
